Burn Treatment for Children in Chicago: What Parents Need to Know

A burn injury is a serious medical event, especially for a child. In Chicago, parents have access to some of the nation’s leading resources for pediatric burn care, but the first and most critical steps happen at home or on the scene. Knowing how to respond quickly and correctly can make a significant difference in your child's recovery.

Immediate First Aid for a Child's Burn

If your child sustains a thermal burn (from heat, such as fire, hot liquid, or a hot surface), the priority is to stop the burning process and prevent further tissue damage .

Remove the child from the heat source .

Cool the burn immediately. Run cool (not cold) water over the affected area for about 5 minutes. Do not use ice, as it can cause additional damage to the injured skin .

Do not apply ointments, powders, oils, or sprays, including butter or grease, to the burned area. These "home remedies" can worsen the injury and increase the risk of infection .

Protect the burn by covering it loosely with a dry, sterile gauze bandage or a clean cloth .

If clothing is stuck to the burn, do not pull it off. Instead, cut the fabric around the burn and leave the stuck portion in place .

Do not break any blisters that have formed, as this can increase the risk of infection .

Red Flags: When to Seek Emergency Care in Chicago

For children, certain burns require immediate medical attention. You should call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room if the burn involves :

The hand, foot, face, eyes, or groin.

A large area of the body.

A burn that goes all the way around an arm or leg.

In all cases, even if a burn appears minor, it is advisable to contact your child's healthcare provider. They can assess if a tetanus shot is needed . For pain, a dose of acetaminophen or ibuprofen appropriate for your child's weight can be given .

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: When should I call the doctor for a minor burn?

Call your pediatrician for any burn that is not a medical emergency. Even for a small burn, it's important to get professional advice to ensure proper healing and to check if a tetanus shot is needed .

Q: How long should I cool a burn with water?

Run cool water over the burn for about 5 minutes. Do not use ice, as this can cause further damage to the injured skin .

Q: What home remedies can I use for a child's burn?

Do not use any home remedies. Avoid applying butter, oils, toothpaste, or any ointments unless prescribed by a doctor. These can worsen the burn and lead to infection .

Q: Should I break a blister on a burn?

No. Do not break any blisters. An intact blister acts as a natural barrier against infection .

Q: When should I go to the ER for a burn?

Go to the ER or call 911 immediately if the burn is on the face, hands, feet, eyes, or groin, or if it covers a large area of the body .

Q: Does my child need a tetanus shot after a burn?

Ask your child's provider if a tetanus shot is necessary. Burns can be a pathway for tetanus, especially if the wound is dirty or deep .

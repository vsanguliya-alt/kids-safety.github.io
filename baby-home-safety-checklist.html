Baby Home Safety Checklist

Creating a safe home environment is one of the most important things you can do for your baby. You can't remove all the safety hazards from your life, but you can reduce them (Source: NIH). This checklist will help you address key safety areas room by room.

General Home Safety

Install and maintain smoke alarms and carbon monoxide detectors (Source: NIH).

Make a first aid kit for your home (Source: NIH).

Make a family emergency plan (Source: NIH).

Keep emergency phone numbers by your telephones (Source: NIH).

Keep guns unloaded and locked up (Source: NIH).

Kitchen

Store knives, scissors, and other sharp objects in locked cabinets or high, out-of-reach drawers.

Keep cleaning supplies, detergents, and other chemicals in a locked cabinet.

Use back burners and turn pot handles inward when cooking.

Keep small appliances (toasters, blenders) unplugged and out of reach.

Install a stove guard.

Keep magnets out of reach.

Living Room

Secure heavy furniture, such as bookcases and TV stands, to the wall.

Use outlet covers on all unused electrical sockets.

Keep electrical cords out of reach and secure them.

Remove small objects (coins, buttons, beads) that could be choking hazards.

Place safety locks on cabinets that contain breakable items.

Bathroom

Store medicines, vitamins, and cosmetics in a locked cabinet.

Keep cleaning supplies out of reach.

Set your water heater to 120°F (49°C) to prevent scalding.

Never leave your child unattended in the bath, even for a moment.

Use a toilet lock to prevent drowning.

Bedroom

Place the crib away from windows, blinds, and curtains.

Use a firm mattress and keep the crib free of pillows, blankets, and soft toys.

Secure furniture to the wall.

Use cordless window coverings.

Stairs and Hallways

Install safety gates at the top and bottom of stairs.

Keep stairs clear of clutter and tripping hazards.

Use non-slip mats on hardwood or tile floors.

Fire Safety

Prevent cooking fires: Don't leave the stove or oven unattended when they are on (Source: NIH).

Supervise appliances: Don't let children use kitchen appliances unsupervised (Source: NIH).

When to See a Doctor

If you are worried about your child's safety, or if you think your child has ingested something poisonous, call your health care provider or poison control immediately.

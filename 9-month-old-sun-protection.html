Sun Protection for Your 9-Month-Old

At 9 months old, your baby's skin is still delicate and more sensitive to the sun's harmful rays. Everyone is at risk for eye damage from the sun year-round (Source: NIH). Since a child's eye lenses are clearer than an adult's, allowing more UV rays to pass through, protecting their eyes from the sun is especially important.

Tips for Sun Protection

Keep Your Baby in the Shade: The most effective way to protect your baby is to keep them in the shade, especially during the peak sun hours of 10 a.m. to 4 p.m. Use a stroller canopy, an umbrella, or a tree for shade.

Use Sunscreen: Choose a broad-spectrum sunscreen with SPF 30 or higher that is water-resistant and formulated for children or sensitive skin. Apply sunscreen 15minutes before going outside and reapply every two hours, or more often if your baby is sweating or swimming. Test a small area of skin first to check for any allergic reaction.

Dress for Sun Protection: Dress your baby in lightweight, long-sleeved shirts and pants. Look for clothing with a UPF (Ultraviolet Protection Factor) rating. Always use a wide-brimmed hat to protect the face, ears, and neck, and protect their eyes with sunglasses that block UVA and UVB rays.

Additional Tips: Be extra careful on cloudy days, as UV rays can still be harmful, and be mindful of reflected light, which can also cause sunburn.

What to Avoid

Do not use sunscreen on infants under 6 months unless recommended by a doctor.

Avoid sunscreens with oxybenzone or retinyl palmitate.

Do not use tanning beds or sunlamps.

When to See a Doctor

If your child gets a sunburn that is severe (blistering), or if they have a fever or signs of dehydration, seek medical attention.

Fall Prevention for Your 6-Month-Old: A Guide for Parents

At 6 months old, your baby is becoming more mobile, learning to roll, sit, and perhaps even starting to scoot or crawl. With these new skills comes an increased risk of falls, making fall prevention a critical part of keeping your baby safe.

Why Babies Are at Risk

A 6-month-old is just beginning to explore their world. They are top-heavy, have limited coordination, and don&#x27;t yet understand what is dangerous. This combination makes falls a common cause of injury.

Common fall hazards include:

Changing Tables and High Surfaces: Changing tables, beds, and sofas are common places for falls.

Baby Gear: High chairs, strollers, and shopping carts can tip over if not used correctly.

Stairs: Stairs become a hazard as soon as your baby starts to move.

Furniture: Climbing on or pulling up on unstable furniture.

Prevention Tips for Your Home

Never Leave Your Baby Unattended on High Surfaces: Always stay within arm&#x27;s reach of your baby on a changing table, bed, or sofa. Even a newborn can roll off a changing table.

Use Safety Straps: Always use the safety straps on high chairs, strollers, and shopping carts. Never leave a baby unattended in a stroller or high chair.

Install Safety Gates: Use safety gates at the top and bottom of stairs as soon as your baby is mobile.

Secure Furniture: Anchor heavy furniture, such as bookshelves and dressers, to the wall to prevent tipping.

Keep the Floor Clear: Remove small rugs, toys, and other tripping hazards from walkways.

Use Window Guards and Locks: Install window guards on all windows that are not emergency exits.

Supervise and Childproof: Don&#x27;t leave small children unattended, and take steps to childproof your home.

What to Do in an Emergency

If your child has a fall, stay calm and assess the situation.

Call 112 or 999 if your child is unconscious, has trouble breathing, or is having a seizure.

Do not move your child if you suspect a neck or back injury.

Apply pressure to any bleeding wounds.

Watch for signs of a head injury, such as vomiting, confusion, or unusual sleepiness.

If you are worried about your child after a fall, even if they seem fine, call your healthcare provider for advice.

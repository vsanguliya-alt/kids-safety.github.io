Baby Fall Prevention in Children 0: Complete Parent Guide

Falls are the leading cause of injury-related hospital visits for young children, with most fall-related injuries among children under five happening right at home . Because babies and toddlers are naturally curious and constantly developing new motor skills, they are especially vulnerable to tumbles from furniture, stairs, and windows . The good news is that most falls are preventable with active supervision and a few key safety adjustments .

Why Young Children Are at High Risk for Falls

A baby’s development happens in fast-forward. As soon as they learn to roll, they can roll off a changing table. Once they can sit, they can topple from a couch. And long before they can walk, they can climb—pulling themselves up on furniture to reach windows or explore unsafe areas . Babies also have large heads compared to their bodies, so when they fall, their head often hits the ground first, increasing the risk of concussion or other brain injuries .

Preventing Falls at Home

Never Leave Babies Unattended on High Surfaces

A baby can wiggle, roll, or fall in a split second—even when you are in the same room. Falls from changing tables, beds, sofas, and counters are among the most common causes of infant head injuries . The AAP advises to always keep one hand on your baby when they are on a changing table or any high surface, even when safety straps are in place . If you need to step away for any reason, move your baby to a safe place like a crib or playpen . For the safest option, consider changing diapers on the floor .

Use Safety Straps on All Equipment

Always buckle your child into highchairs, strollers, swings, and shopping carts, even for short periods . The safest straps go around both the waist and between the legs to prevent sliding . Never place car seats, bouncers, or baby carriers on top of tables, counters, or furniture—the safest place is always the floor .

Stair Gates and Window Guards

Install hardware-mounted safety gates at the top and bottom of stairs. Do not use pressure-mounted gates at the top of stairs, as they are less secure and can tip over . Once your child’s chin reaches the top of the gate or they turn two years old, it’s time to remove the gate—they may start climbing over it .

For windows, install window guards on all windows above the first floor . Do not rely on window screens; a child’s weight can easily push through them . Also use window stops to prevent windows from opening more than 4 inches (10 cm) . Move furniture away from windows to prevent climbing .

Anchor Furniture and Remove Clutter

Secure tall furniture like dressers, bookshelves, and TVs to the wall with anchors or straps . Avoid placing attractive items on top of furniture that might encourage your child to climb . Keep stairs, floors, and walkways clear of toys, clutter, and loose rugs to prevent tripping .

Avoid Baby Walkers and Playground Safety

The AAP and other safety organizations strongly advise against using baby walkers with wheels. They can tip over, fall down stairs, or give children access to dangerous areas and objects . Choose a stationary activity center instead.

When visiting playgrounds, ensure the equipment is age-appropriate (for children under 5, the equipment should be under 4 feet tall) and that the ground surface is soft and shock-absorbing, such as wood chips, rubber, or sand, not grass or concrete . Always supervise your child during play .

When to Call the Doctor

If your child falls and is not acting normally afterward, or if they have a serious fall from a significant height, call your pediatrician or go to the emergency room .

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: When should I call the doctor after my baby falls?

Call your pediatrician or go to the ER if your child has a serious fall, loses consciousness, is not acting normally, or is very sleepy and difficult to wake .

Q: When should I install safety gates?

Install a hardware-mounted safety gate at the top of stairs as soon as your baby starts to move around, usually around 6 months. Use gates at the bottom of stairs once your child begins to climb .

Q: What home remedies help with fall prevention?

The best home remedies are prevention-focused: anchor furniture, use safety gates and window guards, keep one hand on your baby on high surfaces, and always use safety straps on equipment .

Q: Is it normal for toddlers to fall a lot?

Yes, falls are a normal part of learning to walk, run, and climb. However, serious falls can be prevented with proper supervision and safety measures .

Q: Can I use a baby walker with wheels?

No. Baby walkers with wheels are dangerous and have been banned for sale in some countries. They are a leading cause of falls down stairs and injuries .

Q: When should I move my child from a crib to a toddler bed?

Move your child to a toddler bed as soon as they show signs of climbing out of the crib, as this can lead to dangerous falls .

Q: When should I go to the ER for a fall?

Go to the ER immediately if your child has a seizure, is unconscious, is not breathing, is very difficult to wake, or has a serious injury from a high fall.

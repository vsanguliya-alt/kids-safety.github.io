Preventing Furniture Tip-Over Accidents: A Guide for Parents

Furniture tip-over accidents are a serious and preventable cause of injury for young children. Toddlers are naturally curious climbers, and a heavy piece of furniture like a dresser or bookcase can easily tip over if they try to pull themselves up on it.

Why This Is a Danger

Young children are top-heavy and have a high center of gravity. When they pull themselves up on furniture, the weight of their body can easily cause the piece to tip forward. This is especially dangerous for children under 5, who are most likely to be injured in these accidents. The most common injuries from furniture tip-overs are head injuries, which can be severe.

How to Prevent Furniture Tip-Over

Furniture tip-overs are almost entirely preventable. The most important step is to anchor furniture and TVs to the wall.

Anchor Furniture to the Wall

Use furniture anchors (anti-tip straps or brackets) to secure dressers, bookshelves, TV stands, and other heavy furniture to the wall.

Install anchors according to the manufacturer's instructions.

If you have an older piece of furniture, you can purchase furniture anchors at any hardware store.

Additional Prevention Tips

Place heavier items on lower shelves and in lower drawers to lower the center of gravity.

Don't place tempting items like toys or remotes on top of tall furniture that might encourage climbing.

Secure your TV either by mounting it to the wall or placing it on a low, stable stand.

Inspect your furniture regularly to ensure it is stable.

What to Look For

Check your home for furniture that could be a tipping hazard. This includes:

Dressers and chests of drawers

Bookshelves and entertainment centers

Tall, heavy cabinets

Freestanding TVs

Any piece of furniture that a child could climb

Tips for choosing furniture:

Choose furniture with wide bases for stability.

In the UK, look for furniture that complies with safety standard BS EN 14749 .

What to Do in an Emergency

If a piece of furniture tips over on your child, call 112 or 999 immediately. If your child is unconscious or not breathing, start CPR. Do not move your child if you suspect a neck or back injury. Even if your child seems fine, take them to a doctor to be checked.

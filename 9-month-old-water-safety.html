Water Safety for Your 9-Month-Old

At 9 months old, your baby is likely becoming more mobile and curious, which makes water safety a critical concern. Drowning is a leading cause of injury-related death among young children, and it can happen quickly and silently in as little as an inch of water.

Why Water Safety Is Critical

Water presents a constant danger for children of this age. Babies are naturally curious and drawn to water but do not understand the risks. Drowning can occur in bathtubs, buckets, wading pools, toilets, and other containers of water.

Key Safety Measures

Supervision and Barriers: Never leave your baby alone or with older siblings near any water, even for a moment. Maintain constant, attentive supervision at all times. Install a four-sided fence around home swimming pools and use self-closing, self-latching gates. Empty all buckets, wading pools, and other containers immediately after use. Keep bathroom doors closed and use toilet seat locks.

Learn CPR: In an emergency, seconds matter. Knowing how to perform CPR can be the difference between life and death.

Use Life Jackets: When on boats or near natural bodies of water, ensure your child wears a properly fitted life jacket approved by the U.S. Coast Guard. Do not rely on inflatable toys or water wings for safety.

What to Do in an Emergency

If you find your child unresponsive in water, remove them immediately and call for emergency help. Begin CPR if you are trained to do so. The sources emphasize that when someone is choking, quick action is lifesaving; this same principle applies to drowning. Do not use the Heimlich maneuver.

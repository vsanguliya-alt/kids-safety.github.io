Poison Control Number: A Lifeline for Your 18-Month-Old

A poison is any substance that is harmful to your body (Source: NIH). For a curious 18-month-old, almost anything in the home—from cleaning products to medications—can become a poison. Having the Poison Control number readily available is essential for every parent.

The Poison Control Number: 1If you suspect someone has been poisoned, call your local poison control center at 1right away (Source: NIH). This number works from anywhere in the United States and connects you to a poison expert 24 hours a day, 7 days a week.

When to Call Poison Control

Call Poison Control immediately if your child:

Has swallowed any medication, vitamin, or supplement not meant for them.

Has ingested any household cleaner, detergent, or chemical.

Has eaten a plant or berry that you are unsure is safe.

Has gotten any substance in their eyes or on their skin.

Has inhaled fumes or smoke.

You suspect your child has been exposed to any poison.

Do not wait for symptoms to appear. Many poisons can cause serious damage before any signs are visible.

What to Do in a Poisoning Emergency

Stay calm.

Call Poison Control at 1immediately.

Follow the operator&#x27;s instructions. Do not induce vomiting or give your child anything to drink unless the poison expert tells you to.

How to Poison-Proof Your Home

Store all medicines, cleaners, and other dangerous substances in locked cabinets.

Keep the Poison Control number near every phone and enter it into your cell phone.

Never refer to medicine as candy.

When to Call 911

Call 911 if your child:

Is unconscious or not breathing.

Is having a seizure.

Is unable to wake up.

Is having severe difficulty breathing.

Is acting strange or confused.

If you are worried about your child, call your health care provider right away.

Sun Protection for Your 18-Month-Old

Sun protection is essential for an 18-month-old, as their skin is delicate and more sensitive to the sun&#x27;s harmful rays. Everyone is at risk for eye damage from the sun year-round (Source: NIH). Since a child&#x27;s eye lenses are clearer than an adult&#x27;s, allowing more UV rays to pass through, protecting their eyes from the sun is especially important.

Tips for Sun Protection

Keep Your Child in the Shade

The most effective way to protect your child from the sun is to keep them in the shade, especially during the peak sun hours of 10 a.m. to 4 p.m. Use a stroller canopy, an umbrella, or a tree for shade.

Use Sunscreen

Choose a broad-spectrum sunscreen with SPF 30 or higher.

Choose a sunscreen that is water-resistant and specifically formulated for children or sensitive skin.

Apply sunscreen 15minutes before going outside.

Apply to all exposed skin, including the face, hands, ears, and tops of feet.

Reapply every two hours, or more often if your child is sweating or swimming.

Test a small area of your child&#x27;s skin first to check for any allergic reaction.

Dress for Sun Protection

Dress your child in lightweight, long-sleeved shirts and pants.

Look for clothing with a UPF (Ultraviolet Protection Factor) rating.

Wear a wide-brimmed hat to protect the face, ears, and neck.

Protect your child&#x27;s eyes with sunglasses that block UVA and UVB rays.

Additional Tips

Avoid direct sun exposure during peak hours (10 a.m. to 4 p.m.).

Be extra careful on cloudy days, as UV rays can still be harmful.

Be mindful of reflected light, which can also cause sunburn.

What to Avoid

Avoid sunscreens with oxybenzone or retinyl palmitate.

Do not use tanning beds or sunlamps.

When to See a Doctor

If your child gets a sunburn that is severe (blistering), or if they have a fever or signs of dehydration, seek medical attention.

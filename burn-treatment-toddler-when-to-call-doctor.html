Burn Treatment in Toddlers: When to Call the Doctor

First Aid Steps for Minor Burns

If your toddler sustains a burn, immediate first aid is crucial. Immediately cool the burn under cool (not ice-cold) running water for 20 minutes . Remove any clothing, diapers, or jewelry near the burn, unless they are stuck to the skin . If clothing is stuck, do not pull it off; instead, cut around it . Gently wash the area with mild, unscented soap . Cover the burn loosely with a clean, non-fluffy cloth or cling film . Do not pop any blisters, apply ice, or use home remedies like butter or toothpaste, as these can worsen the injury .

When to Call the Doctor or Seek Urgent Care

Seek medical advice if the burn shows any signs of infection such as spreading redness, swelling, warmth, or a foul odor, or if you notice fluid (pus) oozing from the wound . You should also contact your pediatrician if a blistered burn is larger than 2.5 cm , if the pain is not relieved by age-appropriate pain medication , or if the burn has not healed after 14 days . Additionally, any burn that is on the face, hands, feet, or groin area should be assessed by a medical professional .

Emergency Warning Signs

Seek emergency medical care immediately if the burn is large, appears white, charred, or leathery, or if it was caused by fire, electricity, or chemicals . Call emergency services if your child has difficulty breathing, has a fever of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher, or shows signs of shock, such as cold and clammy skin .

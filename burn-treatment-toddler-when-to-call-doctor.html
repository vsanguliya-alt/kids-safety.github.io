Diaper Rash: When to Call the Doctor

Diaper rash is a common inflammatory skin condition in infants, caused by moisture, friction, and prolonged contact with urine and feces . Most cases resolve with home care, but specific signs indicate you should seek medical advice.

🔴 Call Your Pediatrician Immediately If:

Infection Signs

Pus-filled bumps, blisters, ulcers, or sores that ooze or bleed Bright red rash with small red spots around the edges — may indicate a yeast (Candida) infection Red streaks spreading from the rash area Increased pain, swelling, or warmth in the affected area Fever

Your baby has a fever (temperature of 100.4°F/38°C or higher in infants under 3 months) Fever accompanying any rash Worsening or Persistent Rash

Rash gets worse despite 2–3 days of home treatment Rash does not improve after 3 days of home care Rash spreads beyond the diaper area to the abdomen, back, arms, or face Rash is severe or unusual in appearance Severe Discomfort

Rash causes burning or pain when your baby passes urine or stool Your baby is inconsolable or in significant pain Rash bleeds, itches, or oozes fluid 👶 Special Considerations

Infants under 3 months: Any rash with blisters or discoloration requires prompt medical evaluation First 6 weeks after birth: A rash developing during this period should be assessed by a provider Candida (yeast) infection: If your pediatrician confirms this, treatment may require an antifungal cream ✅ What to Do at Home

Change diapers frequently

Allow diaper-free time for air exposure

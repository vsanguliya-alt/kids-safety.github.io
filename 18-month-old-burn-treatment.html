When Should You Worry About a 1-Year-Old's Burn Treatment?

Burns are a common injury in young children, with scalds from hot liquids accounting for nearly 90% of cases . For a 1-year-old, even a small burn can be serious due to their delicate skin and small body size. Here is what pediatricians consider a medical emergency versus a minor injury that can be managed at home.

Emergency Signs: Immediate Medical Attention Required

You should call an ambulance or go to the nearest emergency room immediately if your 1-year-old has a burn with any of the following signs :

Location of the burn: The burn is on the face, neck, hands, feet, or genital area .

Size of the burn: The burn is larger than the size of your child's hand or covers more than 10% of their body .

Breathing problems: Your child has difficulty breathing, is coughing, or has soot around the nose or mouth, which can indicate smoke inhalation .

Severity of the burn: The burn appears white, chalky, or charred (full-thickness burn), or there is deep blistering .

Type of burn: The burn was caused by chemicals or electricity, even if it appears small .

Child's condition: Your child is unresponsive, in shock (cold, clammy skin, weakness), or has a fever above 38°C .

First Aid to Administer Immediately

While waiting for medical help or for minor burns, you should:

Cool the burn: Run cool (not cold) water over the burn for at least 20 minutes as soon as possible . Do not use ice, as it can cause further damage .

Remove constrictions: Gently remove any clothing or jewelry near the burn, unless it is stuck to the skin. If stuck, cut around it and leave it in place .

Cover the burn: After cooling, cover the burn loosely with cling film or a clean, non-fluffy cloth to protect it from infection .

What NOT to Do

Do not apply any creams, ointments, butter, toothpaste, or other home remedies to the burn, as they can worsen the injury or cause infection .

Do not pop any blisters that form, as this increases the risk of infection .

When to Call Your Pediatrician

For anything more serious than a superficial reddening of the skin (sunburn), or if the redness and pain continue for more than a few hours, contact your pediatrician . Also, watch for signs of infection in a healing burn, such as redness, swelling, or pus .

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: When should I call the doctor for my 1-year-old's burn?

Call your pediatrician for any burn that is not a superficial reddening, or if redness, swelling, or pain continues for more than a few hours. You should also call if you see signs of infection like pus or increased redness .

Q: How long should I cool a burn with water?

Run cool water over the burn for at least 20 minutes. It is important to do this as soon as possible after the injury occurs .

Q: What home remedies help a 1-year-old with a burn?

No home remedies should be applied to a burn. Never use butter, oils, powders, or toothpaste. The only first aid is to cool the burn with water and cover it with a clean, non-adhesive dressing .

Q: Can I give my 1-year-old pain medicine for a burn?

For minor burns, you may give acetaminophen for pain if your child has no conditions preventing it. Always use a weight-appropriate dose. For more serious burns, pain management should be directed by a doctor .

Q: Should I break a blister on my child's burn?

No. Do not break or pop any blisters. Intact skin over a blister helps prevent infection .

Q: When should I go to the ER for a burn?

Go to the ER immediately if the burn is on the face, hands, feet, or genitals, is larger than your child's hand, is white or charred, or if your child has difficulty breathing or is unwell .

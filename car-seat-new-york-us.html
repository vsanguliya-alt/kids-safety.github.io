Car Seat Safety in New York: What Parents Must Know

Child car seat safety is a top priority for New York State, governed by strict laws designed to protect your child. Navigating the city's busy streets makes proper car seat use even more critical. Here is what you need to know about the law, best practices, and available resources in New York.

New York State Car Seat Laws

New York law mandates specific requirements based on a child's age and size:

Children under age 2 must ride in a rear-facing car seat.

Children under age 4 must be restrained in a child safety seat that meets federal standards.

Children ages 4 through 7 must be secured in an appropriate child restraint system, which can include a forward-facing car seat with a harness or a booster seat, until their 8th birthday.

The law requires that the restraint system be appropriate for the child's size and weight according to the manufacturer's instructions. Children under 13 should ride in the back seat whenever possible. Violations of these laws can result in a civil fine of up to $50 for a first offense.

Best Practices: Beyond the Law

Pediatricians and safety experts emphasize going beyond the minimum legal requirements. The "best practice" is to keep your child in a rear-facing car seat for as long as possible, until they reach the maximum height or weight limit allowed by the seat manufacturer, which is often well beyond age 2. This position provides the best protection for a child's developing head, neck, and spine in a crash. After outgrowing the rear-facing seat, a child should use a forward-facing seat with a five-point harness until reaching that seat's limits, then transition to a belt-positioning booster seat until the adult seat belt fits properly (typically when the child is about 4 feet 9 inches tall and between 8 and 12 years old).

Finding Help in New York

Installing a car seat correctly is challenging. Studies show that nearly 90% of car seats are installed incorrectly. Fortunately, New York offers many free resources to help.

NYC DOT Car Seat Fittings: The New York City Department of Transportation (DOT) offers free car seat inspections at various locations throughout the city during the warmer months (April through October), as well as virtual checks year-round. Appointments are required and can be made by calling (347) 712or emailing carseats@dot.nyc.gov.

New York State Police: The New York State Police also offer free car seat safety checks by certified technicians. Appointments can be made by calling (631) 756or emailing LPIO@troopers.ny.gov.

Safe Kids Coalitions: Organizations like Safe Kids NYC, led by the NYC DOT, and Safe Kids Nassau County provide car seat education and fitting stations.

Other Locations: The Greenburgh Police Department in Westchester County has a full-time Child Safety Seat Inspection Station, and many other local organizations offer similar services.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: When should I switch my child from a rear-facing to a forward-facing car seat?

You should keep your child in a rear-facing seat for as long as possible, until they reach the weight or height limit set by the car seat manufacturer, which is often beyond age 2. New York State law requires rear-facing until age 2.

Q: What is the car seat law in New York for taxis and ride-shares?

While New York law requires child restraints in most vehicles, proposed legislation has sought to extend these requirements to taxis and liveries, though specifics can vary. It is always safest to use a car seat or booster seat in any vehicle.

Q: Where can I get my car seat inspected for free in NYC?

You can get a free car seat inspection at a NYC DOT fitting station (by appointment) or through the New York State Police.

Q: Is it safe to use a second-hand car seat?

Generally, it is best to avoid used car seats unless you know the complete history. Never use a seat that is expired, has visible damage, or has been in a moderate or severe crash.

Q: My child is over 8 years old. Do they still need a car seat?

Once a child is over 8, they must be restrained by a seat belt that fits them properly. The lap belt should sit low across the hips, and the shoulder belt across the chest.

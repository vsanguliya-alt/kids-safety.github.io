Safe Medicine Storage for Your 9-Month-Old

At 9 months old, your baby is likely becoming increasingly mobile—crawling, pulling up, and exploring their environment with their hands and mouth. This makes secure medicine storage critical.

The Danger of Unsecured Medications

Medications—including prescription drugs, over-the-counter medicines, vitamins, and herbal supplements—are a leading cause of accidental poisoning in children. A 9-month-old is especially vulnerable because they are curious and don't understand what is dangerous. They may mistake colorful pills or sweet-flavored liquids for candy or juice, leading to ingestion.

How to Store Medications Safely

Choose a Secure Location: Store all medicines in a locked cabinet or box that is high up and out of your child's reach. Never leave medications on countertops, nightstands, in purses, or in bags where a child can find them.

Use Child-Resistant Packaging: Always keep medications in their original child-resistant containers. Make sure the cap is tightly secured after each use. Remember that child-resistant does not mean child-proof; it only means it takes longer for a child to open.

Never Refer to Medicine as Candy: Avoid calling medicine "candy" to make it more appealing. Teach your child that only adults give medicine.

Dispose of Unused Medications Safely: Dispose of expired or unused medications properly. You can often take them to a pharmacy or police station for safe disposal. Do not flush them down the toilet unless the label specifically instructs you to.

In Case of Emergency

Keep the Poison Control number (1in the US) near every phone and enter it into your cell phone.

If you suspect your child has swallowed any medication, call Poison Control immediately. Do not wait for symptoms to appear.

If your child is unconscious, having trouble breathing, or having a seizure, call 911.

Button Battery Danger for Your 3-Year-Old: A Silent Emergency

Button batteries (also known as coin cell batteries) are the small, round, silver batteries found in many household items like remote controls, key fobs, watches, and electronic toys. For a curious 3-year-old, these objects are the perfect size to put in their mouth, nose, or ears, creating a life-threatening emergency.

Why Button Batteries Are So Dangerous

When a button battery gets stuck in a child&#x27;s esophagus (the tube connecting the mouth to the stomach), a very serious injury can occur in a short amount of time. The battery&#x27;s electrical current interacts with bodily fluids to create hydroxide, which is a caustic alkali. This chemical reaction can burn and eat through the tissue of the esophagus in as little as two hours, causing severe internal injuries, bleeding, and even death.

The danger is that the initial injury from a lodged battery can be painless, so your child might not show any obvious signs of distress right away. Symptoms of a swallowed button battery can look like a common illness and may include:

Coughing, drooling, or gagging

Difficulty swallowing or refusing to eat

Chest pain or stomach pain

Fever, vomiting, or breathing difficulties

What to Do If You Suspect Your Child Has Swallowed a Battery

If you think your 3-year-old has swallowed a button battery, do not wait for symptoms to appear. This is a medical emergency.

1. Call Emergency Services Immediately

Call 911 (or your local emergency number) immediately. Do not delay.

2. Do Not Induce Vomiting

Never try to make your child vomit, and do not give them anything to eat or drink unless specifically instructed by a medical professional.

3. Do Not Perform the Heimlich Maneuver

The Heimlich maneuver is for choking, not for a battery that has been swallowed and passed to the esophagus. Trying it could cause more damage.

4. Go to the Emergency Room

Take your child to the nearest emergency department. If it is safe to do so, bring the battery package or a similar battery to show the doctor exactly what was swallowed. Hospitals have special protocols for quickly diagnosing and removing lodged batteries.

How to Prevent Battery Ingestion

Prevention is the best strategy. Keep all devices with button batteries out of your child&#x27;s reach and sight. Secure battery compartments with tape to make them harder to open, and never leave loose batteries around the house. Check your home for devices that contain these batteries and be especially cautious with products that are not child-resistant.

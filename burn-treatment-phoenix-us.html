Burn Treatment for Children in Phoenix: What Parents Need to Know

Burn injuries in children are a serious concern. For parents in Phoenix, the first critical steps happen at home, but the city is also home to the Diane & Bruce Halle Arizona Burn Center, the first and only nationally verified adult and pediatric burn center in the state . Knowing how to respond and where to go is essential for your child’s recovery.

Step 1: Immediate First Aid for a Burn

If your child sustains a burn, act quickly to stop the burning process and minimize further tissue damage .

Remove the child from the heat source.

Cool the burn: Run cool (not cold) water over the burn for 5 to 20 minutes to reduce pain and prevent scarring . Do not use ice, as this can cause additional injury .

Remove clothing and jewelry near the burn, unless they are stuck to the skin. If stuck, leave them in place .

Do not apply any ointments, butter, oils, or "home remedies" to the burn. These can worsen the injury and increase the risk of infection .

Protect the burn by covering it loosely with a sterile, non-stick gauze bandage or clean cloth . Do not use adhesive bandages directly on the burn.

Do not break any blisters. An intact blister acts as a natural barrier against infection .

Step 2: Red Flags — When to Seek Emergency Care in Phoenix

For children, many burns require immediate medical attention at a specialized burn center or an emergency department. Pediatric burns are the most common in children under 5 years of age, with scalding (from hot water, soups, or bath water) being a frequent cause .

Call 911 or go to the nearest emergency department if the burn has any of the following characteristics :

Location: The burn is on the face, neck, hands, feet, genitals, or over a major joint.

Size: The burn is larger than the size of your child's hand.

Appearance: The burn is blistered or appears white, waxy, leathery, brown, or charred (which indicates a deep, full-thickness burn) .

Cause: The burn was caused by electricity or chemicals .

Associated symptoms: Your child has difficulty breathing, was involved in a fire (smoke inhalation), or shows signs of shock .

After treatment, home care is essential for healing. Your child may experience itching during the healing process, which is common but can be managed with prescribed creams, antihistamines, or cold compresses .

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: When should I call the doctor for a minor burn?

Call your pediatrician for any burn that is not a medical emergency. Even for a small burn, professional advice can ensure proper healing and help you determine if a tetanus shot is needed .

Q: How long should I cool a burn with water?

Run cool water over the burn for 5 to 20 minutes. Do not use ice, as this can cause further damage to the injured skin .

Q: What home remedies can I use for a child's burn?

Do not use any home remedies. Avoid butter, oils, toothpaste, or any ointments unless prescribed by a doctor, as these can worsen the burn and lead to infection .

Q: Should I break a blister on a burn?

No. Do not break any blisters. An intact blister acts as a natural barrier against infection .

Q: When should I go to the ER for a burn in Phoenix?

Go to the ER or call 911 immediately if the burn is on the face, hands, feet, or genitals; covers a large area; is blistered; is white or charred; or if your child has trouble breathing .

Safe Sleep in New York: Guidelines for Every Sleep

Ensuring your baby sleeps safely is one of the most important things you can do as a parent. In New York State, the guidelines are clear and are backed by public health authorities to prevent tragic, and often avoidable, infant deaths.

The Problem: Sleep-Related Infant Deaths in New York

Sleep-related infant deaths are a persistent public health issue. Each year in New York State, more than 120 infants die from sleep-related causes, including Sudden Infant Death Syndrome (SIDS), accidental suffocation, and strangulation in bed . These are tragedies that public health officials emphasize are preventable . In fact, New York City reported 185 sleep-related injury deaths among infants under one year old between 2016 and 2020, a rate of 32.9 deaths per 100,000 live births .

The ABCs of Safe Sleep

To combat these risks, New York State promotes a simple, memorable guideline known as the ABCs of Safe Sleep, which has been updated to include a fourth component: A, B, C, and S .

A - Alone: Babies should always sleep alone. They should not share a bed with adults, other children, or pets . Nothing else should be in the crib, including pillows, blankets, stuffed animals, or bumper pads . If you bring your baby into your bed for feeding or comfort, place them back in their own crib before you go back to sleep .

B - Back: Always place your baby on their back to sleep, for every nap and at night . The "Back to Sleep" campaign has been credited with significantly reducing SIDS rates. Once a baby can consistently roll over from back to tummy and back again on their own, it is safe to let them stay in their chosen sleep position .

C - Crib: Babies should sleep in a safety-approved crib, bassinet, or portable play yard. Use a firm, flat mattress with a fitted sheet . It is crucial not to use drop-side cribs, as federal safety standards no longer allow them to be sold or manufactured due to safety hazards . Do not let your baby sleep on adult beds, couches, armchairs, car seats, or other carriers .

S - Smoke-Free Home: No one should smoke around your baby. Exposure to secondhand smoke significantly increases the risk of SIDS and other sleep-related deaths . This includes smoking in the home, in a vehicle, or outdoors near the baby.

Additional Recommendations

Room-Share, Don't Bed-Share: The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) recommends room-sharing—placing your baby's crib or bassinet in your bedroom—for at least the first six months. This can reduce the risk of SIDS by as much as 50% and is much safer than bed-sharing .

Prevent Overheating: Dress your baby lightly for sleep. Use a one-piece sleeper or a wearable blanket (sleep sack) instead of loose blankets. Keep the room at a comfortable temperature and watch for signs of overheating, such as sweating or feeling hot to the touch .

Offer a Pacifier: Consider offering a pacifier for sleep time. Research suggests pacifiers can help lower the risk of SIDS .

Follow the Latest Guidelines: Always ensure your baby's sleep products, including cribs and bassinets, meet current safety standards from the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) and are not subject to recall .

By consistently following the ABCs of safe sleep, families and caregivers can take the most critical steps to protect their infants and ensure a safe and sound night's sleep.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: When should I call the doctor about my baby's sleep?

Call your pediatrician if you have questions about your baby's sleep habits, their breathing during sleep, or if you need help choosing a safe sleep space. For any medical emergency, such as difficulty breathing, call 911 immediately.

Q: What should I do if my baby falls asleep in a car seat or swing?

Move your baby to a firm, flat sleep surface on their back as soon as possible. Sitting devices like car seats, swings, and strollers are not safe for routine sleep .

Q: Are sleep positioners or heart rate monitors recommended?

No. The AAP advises against using products or devices that claim to lower the risk of SIDS, such as sleep positioners (wedges or incliners) or monitors that detect heart rate and breathing. No known products can effectively do this, and they can be unsafe .

Q: Can my baby sleep on their tummy during the day?

Yes, but only for tummy time, which is supervised playtime while your baby is awake. This helps develop strong shoulder and neck muscles .

Q: Is it safe to use a second-hand crib?

Only if you are certain of its full history and are confident that it has not been recalled. Use the Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) website to check for recalls. Never use a crib that is damaged, has missing parts, or is over ten years old. Older cribs often do not meet modern safety standards .

Q: Can I get help getting a safe crib?

Yes. The NYS Office of Children and Family Services (OCFS) provides "Pack N Play" portable cribs to local agencies for families who may not have the resources to obtain them. Check with your local Social Services agency or the HEARS Family Line at 1HEARS for more information .

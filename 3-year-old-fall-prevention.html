Fall Prevention for Your 3-Year-Old: A Guide for Parents

At 3 years old, your child is becoming more confident on their feet, running, climbing, and exploring their world with increasing independence. This newfound mobility comes with an increased risk of falls, making fall prevention a critical part of keeping your child safe.

Why Preschoolers Are at Risk

A 3-year-old is still top-heavy, unsteady, and doesn&#x27;t yet have a good sense of what is dangerous or how to land safely. They are also driven by curiosity and a desire to explore, which often leads them to climb on furniture and playground equipment. This combination makes falls a common cause of injury.

Common fall hazards include:

Furniture: Climbing on chairs, sofas, beds, and desks.

Stairs: Stairs remain a major hazard.

Playground Equipment: Slides, climbing structures, and monkey bars.

Windows and Balconies: Unsecured windows and balcony railings are serious fall risks.

Uneven Surfaces: Tripping on rugs, toys, or uneven pavement.

Prevention Tips for Your Home

Install Safety Gates: Use safety gates at the top and bottom of stairs, especially for younger children.

Secure Furniture: Anchor heavy furniture, such as bookshelves and dressers, to the wall to prevent tipping.

Use Window Guards and Locks: Install window guards on all windows that are not emergency exits.

Keep the Floor Clear: Remove small rugs, toys, and other tripping hazards from walkways.

Supervise and Childproof: Don&#x27;t leave small children unattended, and take steps to childproof your home.

Playground Safety

Choose age-appropriate equipment: Ensure the playground is suitable for your child&#x27;s age.

Supervise your child: Stay close and watch your child closely.

Check the surface: Look for soft surfaces like wood chips, sand, or rubber mats.

What to Do in an Emergency

If your child has a fall, stay calm and assess the situation.

Call 112 or 999 if your child is unconscious, has trouble breathing, or is having a seizure.

Do not move your child if you suspect a neck or back injury.

Apply pressure to any bleeding wounds.

Watch for signs of a head injury, such as vomiting, confusion, or unusual sleepiness.

If you are worried about your child after a fall, even if they seem fine, call your healthcare provider for advice.

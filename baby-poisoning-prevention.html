Baby Poisoning Prevention: A Parent's Guide

A poison is any substance that is harmful to your body. Poisons can include prescription or over-the-counter medicines taken in doses that are too high, household products like laundry powder, and metals such as lead . Preventing poisoning starts with safe storage and awareness.

Common Poisons in the Home

Medications: Prescription drugs, over-the-counter medicines, vitamins, and supplements.

Household Products: Cleaning supplies, detergents, and chemicals.

Cosmetics and Personal Care Items.

Household Chemicals: Paint thinner, antifreeze, and batteries.

Plants: Some common houseplants and outdoor plants are poisonous.

Carbon monoxide from gas appliances.

Prevention Tips

Store all poisons safely: Store medicines, cleaners and other dangerous substances in locked cabinets. Use child-resistant packaging, and remember that child-resistant does not mean child-proof.

Keep the Poison Control number handy: Call your local poison control center right away if you suspect someone has been poisoned.

Read product labels: Always read and follow the instructions on product labels.

Educate your child: Never refer to medicine as candy.

Be aware of lead hazards: Lead poisoning can be a risk, especially in older homes. If you are concerned, contact your local health department about testing.

What to Do in a Poisoning Emergency

If you suspect your child has been poisoned, stay calm and act quickly.

Call Poison Control immediately. Do not wait for symptoms to appear.

Do not induce vomiting unless a poison expert tells you to.

Do not give your child anything to eat or drink unless instructed.

Follow the operator's instructions.

When to Call 911

Call 911 if your child:

Is unconscious or not breathing.

Is having a seizure.

Is unable to wake up.

Is having severe difficulty breathing.

Is acting strange or confused.

If you are worried about your child, call your health care provider right away.

Choking Hazards for Your 6-Month-Old: A Guide for Parents

At 6 months old, your baby is becoming more curious and is likely starting to explore the world by putting things in their mouth. Young children are at an especially high risk of choking (Source: NIH). Understanding which foods and objects are dangerous and how to prevent accidents is crucial.

High-Risk Foods to Avoid

At 6 months, babies are just beginning to eat solids and are still learning to chew and swallow. Their airways are small, and they don't have molars to grind food. The following foods are considered high-risk choking hazards and should be avoided or prepared with extreme caution.

Whole Nuts and Peanuts: These are hard and round, making them easy to get lodged in a child's airway.

Hot Dogs: The shape and texture of hot dogs are a perfect fit for a child's airway, making them one of the leading causes of choking.

Grapes and Cherry Tomatoes: Whole, round, and firm, these can easily block an airway.

Raw Carrots and Hard Fruits: These are hard and difficult for a baby to chew.

Popcorn and Hard Candies: These can be easily inhaled.

Non-Food Choking Hazards

Choking risks aren't limited to food. Small household objects can also be dangerous.

Small Toys and Toy Pieces: Always choose toys appropriate for your child's age.

Safe Food Preparation Techniques

Mash or puree foods: Foods should be soft, smooth, and easily mashed with the gums.

Cut food into small pieces: Round foods should be cut into small, manageable pieces.

Cook foods until soft: Hard fruits and vegetables should be steamed, boiled, or baked until they are soft.

Remove pits and seeds: Always remove pits and seeds from fruits.

Important Safety Tips

Supervise your child while they are eating.

Make sure your child is sitting up while eating, not lying down.

Avoid giving your baby honey before they are 1 year old.

Avoid cow's milk before age 1.

Learn the Heimlich maneuver and CPR for infants and children.

When to See a Doctor

If your child is choking, it is a medical emergency. Call 112 or 999 immediately. Learning the Heimlich maneuver and CPR can be lifesaving.

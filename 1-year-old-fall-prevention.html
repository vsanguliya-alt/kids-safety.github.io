Fall Prevention for Your 1-Year-Old: A Guide for Parents

At 1 year old, your child is likely becoming more mobile, pulling to stand, and maybe even taking their first steps. With these new skills comes an increased risk of falls, making fall prevention a critical part of keeping your toddler safe.

Why Toddlers Are at Risk

A 1-year-old is just beginning to explore their world on two feet. They are top-heavy, unsteady, and don't yet have a good sense of what is dangerous or how to land safely. This combination makes falls a common cause of injury.

Common fall hazards include:

Furniture: Climbing on chairs, sofas, beds, and changing tables.

Stairs: Stairs are a major hazard for a new walker.

Baby Gear: High chairs, strollers, and shopping carts can tip over if not used correctly.

Windows and Balconies: Unsecured windows and balcony railings are serious fall risks.

Uneven Surfaces: Tripping on rugs, toys, or uneven pavement.

Prevention Tips for Your Home

Install Safety Gates: Use safety gates at the top and bottom of stairs.

Secure Furniture: Anchor heavy furniture, such as bookshelves and dressers, to the wall to prevent tipping.

Use Window Guards and Locks: Install window guards on all windows that are not emergency exits.

Keep the Floor Clear: Remove small rugs, toys, and other tripping hazards from walkways.

Never Leave a Baby Unattended on High Surfaces: Always stay within arm's reach of your child on a changing table, bed, or sofa.

Use Safety Straps: Always use the safety straps on high chairs, strollers, and shopping carts.

Supervise and Childproof: Don't leave small children unattended, and take steps to childproof your home.

What to Do in an Emergency

If your child has a fall, stay calm and assess the situation.

Call 112 or 999 if your child is unconscious, has trouble breathing, or is having a seizure.

Do not move your child if you suspect a neck or back injury.

Apply pressure to any bleeding wounds.

Watch for signs of a head injury, such as vomiting, confusion, or unusual sleepiness.

If you are worried about your child after a fall, even if they seem fine, call your healthcare provider for advice.

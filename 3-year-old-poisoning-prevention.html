Poisoning Prevention for Your 3-Year-Old

A poison is any substance that is harmful to your body (Source: NIH). At 3 years old, your child is curious, mobile, and exploring the world with their hands and mouth. This makes them especially vulnerable to accidental poisoning.

Common Poisons in the Home

Poisons can include prescription or over-the-counter medicines taken in doses that are too high (Source: NIH). Other common household poisons include:

Medications: Prescription drugs, over-the-counter medicines, vitamins, and supplements.

Cleaning Products: Household cleaners, detergents, and bleach.

Cosmetics and Personal Care Items: Perfume, nail polish remover, and mouthwash.

Pesticides and Insecticides: Bug sprays, weed killers, and rat poison.

Household Chemicals: Paint thinner, antifreeze, and batteries.

Plants: Some common houseplants and outdoor plants are poisonous.

Prevention Tips

Store All Poisons Safely

Store medicines, cleaners and other dangerous substances in locked cabinets.

Use child-resistant packaging, and remember that child-resistant does not mean child-proof.

Keep the Poison Control Number Handy

Call your local poison control center at 1right away if you suspect someone has been poisoned (Source: NIH).

Enter the number into your cell phone and post it near every phone.

Read Product Labels

Always read and follow the instructions on product labels.

Use child-resistant packaging, and remember that child-resistant does not mean child-proof.

Educate Your Child

Never refer to medicine as candy.

What to Do in a Poisoning Emergency

If you suspect your child has been poisoned, stay calm and act quickly.

Call Poison Control at 1immediately.

Do not induce vomiting unless a poison expert tells you to.

Do not give your child anything to eat or drink unless instructed.

Follow the operator's instructions.

When to Call 911

Call 911 if your child:

Is unconscious or not breathing.

Is having a seizure.

Is unable to wake up.

Is having severe difficulty breathing.

Is acting strange or confused.

If you are worried about your child, call your health care provider right away.
